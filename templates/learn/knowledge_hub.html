MODULE 1

Microbial Growth Kinetics & OD600

In wet-lab microbiology, counting billions of individual bacterial cells under a microscope is impractical. Instead, we use spectrophotometry at 600 nanometers (OD600).

Why 600 nm? Light Scattering vs. Absorption

Bacterial cells do not absorb yellow-orange light (600 nm). Instead, they scatter light. A photodetector measures how much light failed to pass straight through. Higher turbidity means higher bacterial cell biomass.

The 4 Growth Curve Phases:
  1. Lag Phase: Microbes adapt to nutrients and synthesize enzymes; zero net division.
  2. Log (Exponential) Phase: Maximum specific division rate ($\mu_{max}$). Cells double at constant intervals ($t_d$).
  3. Stationary Phase: Nutrients deplete and metabolic wastes accumulate. Division equals death rate ($N \to K$).
  4. Decline (Death) Phase: Toxic byproducts exceed tolerance limits; viable cell count declines.
MODULE 2

The Verhulst Logistic Differential Model

Unchecked microbial growth would be exponential ($N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$), which is physically impossible in a closed Petri dish or flask. In 1838, Pierre-François Verhulst introduced carrying capacity constraints.

N(t) = K / [ 1 + ((K - N₀) / N₀) · e^(-rt) ]
Closed-Form Solution to dN/dt = r·N·(1 - N/K)
N₀: Initial Inoculum at $t=0$ (e.g. 0.05 OD).
K: Carrying Capacity plateau (e.g. 1.80 OD).
r: Intrinsic growth velocity parameter ($\text{hr}^{-1}$).
t_d: Doubling time $= \ln(2) / r \approx 0.69315 / r$.
Viva Defense Key: SciPy fits these 3 parameters using non-linear least squares optimization (`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`).
MODULE 3

Kirby-Bauer AST & EUCAST Breakpoints

Antibiotic Susceptibility Testing (AST) determines which antimicrobial agents inhibit pathogen growth. In the Kirby-Bauer assay:

  • Bacteria are spread evenly on Mueller-Hinton agar.
  • Filter paper disks with known drug concentrations are placed on the plate.
  • As drug diffuses outward, a circular clear halo forms (Zone of Inhibition, in mm).
Phenotype Clinical Meaning Zone Size
Sensitive (S) High likelihood of clinical treatment success Large Diameter ($\ge 20\text{mm}$)
Intermediate (I) Uncertain efficacy; buffer zone against errors Moderate Diameter ($15-19\text{mm}$)
Resistant (R) Pathogen grows despite drug; treatment fails Small or Zero ($\le 14\text{mm}$)
DATA SCIENCE HIGHLIGHT

Target Leakage (Variant A vs. Variant B)

This is the most critical concept to explain during your BCA Data Science viva voce examination:

Variant A (Leaked)

Features: Organism, Drug, Temp, pH, Zone Diameter.

Accuracy: ~99-100%

Why it's flawed: Zone diameter directly dictates S/I/R by definition! The model isn't learning biology; it's looking at the answer sheet.

Variant B (Genuine)

Features: Organism, Drug, Temp, pH, Concentration.

Accuracy: ~70-85% (Realistic)

Why it's genuine: Predicts susceptibility before running the assay, providing true predictive power.

Evaluator Defense: "Our ML lab intentionally compares Variant A and Variant B side-by-side to expose the reality of target leakage in clinical data science."
